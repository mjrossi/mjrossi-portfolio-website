About

I grew up outside Detroit in a world built entirely around cars. Taking my first flight at seventeen — into cities where you could actually walk somewhere — reframed how I thought about movement, density, and daily life. Traveling through Chicago, New York, and Seoul deepened that shift into something more sustained: a long-running engagement with how transportation shapes cities and the people who live in them.

I spent thirteen years as a software engineer, working across fintech, financial services, and cloud infrastructure. That work taught me how complex systems actually function — and fail — under real constraints, and how reliability, governance, and stakeholder alignment matter as much as the underlying technology. It also showed me where purely technical solutions run out: where policy, planning, and institutional frameworks have to carry the load.

I'm now channeling that background and a lifelong interest in urban mobility into formal graduate study, and eventually into professional practice at the intersection of policy, systems thinking, and applied data analysis.

Now

Currently a Senior Software Engineer at Heroku (Salesforce) in New York, building distributed credential and authorization infrastructure for next-generation cloud platforms.

Starting autumn 2026, I'll be a full-time master's student in the EIT Urban Mobility Master's Programme in Sustainable Urban Mobility Transitions (SUMT) — a two-year double degree programme. First year at Técnico Lisboa in Lisbon, Portugal; second year at UPC BarcelonaTech in Barcelona, Spain.

Urban Mobility

My engagement with urbanism developed well before I decided to study it formally. Living in Chicago put me in contact with the civic-tech community through ChiHackNight, where technologists worked on open-data projects aimed at urban systems. I contributed a small Ruby library integrating with the Chicago Transit Authority's real-time bus API, and explored GTFS feed structures as a way of understanding how transit agencies expose and organize their operational data.

I've also been involved in transit advocacy through organizations including the Rail Passengers Association, Riders Alliance, and Transportation Alternatives — work that sits alongside sustained reading on mobility, urban history, and the policy and governance frameworks that shape how cities move.
